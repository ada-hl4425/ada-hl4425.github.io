Research Experience

Urban Emission Inversion · Independent Research2025 - Present
Physics-Guided Source Estimation under Meteorological Variability
  • Formulated urban emission-source estimation as an inverse problem, using sparse surface observations to infer latent source parameters under changing meteorological conditions.
  • Developed a physics-regularized neural modelling workflow that embeds physical consistency into learning rather than relying on unconstrained black-box prediction.
  • Developed RE-BAE, a Bayesian approximation-error method that builds a structured representativeness-error covariance from fine-minus-coarse wind differences, and evaluated posterior calibration (coverage, CRPS, sharpness) with real winds over Los Angeles and Dallas–Fort Worth.
  • Examined how monitoring-network density interacts with correlated representativeness error, showing that when this correlation is ignored, added monitors narrow posterior intervals without improving accuracy, so that coverage of nominal 90% intervals falls below 20%.
  • Applied sensitivity analysis across observation constraints to assess the robustness of source estimates and inform monitoring-network design.

Imperial College London · Independent Research ProjectJun 2026 - Sep 2026
Separating Rainfall-Associated PM₂.₅ Reductions from Normal Temporal Change: A Five-Year Matched-Control Study in Tropical SingaporeSupervisors: Dr Yves Plancherel; Dr Fangxin Fang
  • Analysed five years of hourly rainfall, PM₂.₅, and meteorological data across Singapore's five reporting regions to separate rainfall-associated removal from normal temporal change.
  • Matched rainfall events to rain-free controls by region, month, local onset hour, weekday/weekend type, event duration, and pre-rain PM₂.₅; 1,436 of 2,116 high-PM₂.₅ events obtained an eligible matched control (67.9% matching rate).
  • Estimated an excess reduction of 2.49 µg/m³ (95% CI: 2.00-2.98), with a conservative adjusted estimate of 2.08 µg/m³ (95% CI: 1.49-2.67); effects increased with pre-rain PM₂.₅, rainfall duration, and mean intensity.
  • Showed that prediction intervals for individual events crossed zero, supporting inference on the mean effect across many events rather than deterministic claims for a single rainfall event. Thesis repository
